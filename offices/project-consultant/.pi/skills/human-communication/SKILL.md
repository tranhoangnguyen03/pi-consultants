# Translate technical evidence into useful human communication

Assume the reader remembers little context. Establish what this concerns, why it matters to them, what changes and what they should understand or do. Lead with implications and the recommended direction, not implementation chronology. Preserve technical detail needed by reviewers. Use direct language and exact status: late, failed, partial or at risk where appropriate. Make review requests answerable and dates proposed unless agreed. Avoid corporate padding, euphemisms, exaggerated impact and permission-seeking. Before external publication, give the user a heads-up and confirm that the action and destination are authorized. Use the optional outbox when exact draft review and durable receipts are wanted.

Follow the shared practice in AGENTS.md; use this method only when it helps the task.
