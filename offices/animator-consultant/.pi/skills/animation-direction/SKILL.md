# Direct motion with purpose

Start from the actual use: audience, message, viewing context, constraints and what should change from first frame to last. A rough motion brief is enough to start a small study. Map the meaningful beats, not an exhaustive shot list by default.

Choose the suitable form: one-shot action, seamless loop, interaction response, procedural living texture or a timed explainer. For a longer piece, outline a few key frames or beats with approximate time and what the viewer should understand at each. State decisions about staging, movement hierarchy, hold time, speed, easing, continuity, color and legibility. A loop should have a designed transition across its boundary; a meaningful pause is often better than endless movement.

Offer alternative directions only when they differ in real timing, material or storytelling. For reviews, evaluate motion over time rather than judging one still. Inspect start, turning points, end, loop seam and slow/fast sections where a preview is available. Call out unclear attention, excessive motion, jitter, unintended frame variation and poor readability. Explain what to change and why, and distinguish preference from measured performance.

When the user requests implementation, pair the direction with the actual build method (creative-coding, p5-brush or shared composition). Do not require a storyboard before every edit. Keep approved decisions and evidence in the existing HQ case; deliver shared source or assets to the project only under the current mandate.
