# Plan research that answers the decision

Start from the decision the research must inform and what would change it; if no answer would change the decision, say so and skip the study. Check existing evidence first: analytics, support conversations, the research library, past studies and the case. Choose the cheapest method that answers the question with enough confidence: analytics or recordings for what happens, interviews for why and context, usability tests for whether people can complete tasks, surveys only for sizing something already understood, agent trials (real agents on real tasks, trajectories recorded) for how agent users fare. Define who to learn from and why, sample size and its limits, recruiting route, and the questions or tasks. Write neutral, open interview and task prompts; avoid leading questions, hypotheticals and asking people to design the solution. Plan how findings will be recorded and when the decision is due. Recruiting or contacting people is an external action: give the user a heads-up and use only authorized channels. Useful tools: analytics, Dovetail and Intercom connectors, web search for prior research, docparser for existing reports.

Follow the shared practice in AGENTS.md; use this method only when it helps the task.
